# Voice Guide v1: Joe C. Hawley
Applies to all public copy on joechawley.com/research.
Built from the author's own writing: a case study (research register) and a published first-person essay (personal register). Editorial style from the essay's publisher is excluded.

## Two registers
1. Research and analysis (project pages, artifacts, framework text).
2. Personal (About page, journal reflections).

## Research register
- Short sentences, usually 8-15 words, one idea each.
- Active voice; the subject acts. "The attacker got in through an external network device."
- Plain verbs: get in, check, find, fix, decide, own, lead, test.
- State the point, then one limit if needed. "But without testing this path, we cannot be sure."
- Concrete: numbers, examples, who leads, by when, what done looks like ("We are done when...").
- Lead with purpose or the bottom line. Explain why it matters in business terms.
- Lists for steps and cadences; prose for reasoning.
- Define terms once, plainly.

## Personal register
- First person "I". Honest about doubts and turning points.
- Short narrative arc: where I started, what changed, what I learned, what I hope others take from it.
- Credit people who helped, without naming them.
- End with an invitation or a practical takeaway for the reader.
- Warm, not sentimental. One plain sentence over a polished phrase.

## Both registers
- "I" for the research. "We" only for an organization.
- No em or en dashes.
- No clichés or filler: lens, foundational, initiative, leverage, examine, robust, nuanced, emerging body of, pivotal, gold standard, unlock potential, game changer.
- No academic hedging stacks. No noun piles.
- US spelling.

## Before and after
Before: "It examines the Business Information Security Officer role through the lens of organizational governance theory, boundary-spanning leadership, and enterprise risk management."
After: "This project looks at how the BISO role helps business and security leaders make better risk decisions."

Before: "Security organizations struggle to show realized business value in terms leaders recognize."
After: "Security teams do a lot of work. Leaders rarely see what it was worth to the business."
